MEDIEVAL TAVERN / RESTAURANT MANAGEMENT GAME
Working Design Notes / First Demo Plan

==================================================
1. CORE GAME IDEA
==================================================

The game is a combination of:

- RimWorld
- Prison Architect
- Dwarf Fortress
- Tavern / restaurant management games
- Shop management games
- Papa's Pizzeria-style active gameplay
- Penguin Diner-style service flow

Target platforms:
- PC
- Android

The central idea is a medieval tavern / restaurant management game where the player can operate at two levels:

1. MANAGEMENT / COLONY SIMULATION
   - Build rooms
   - Expand the tavern
   - Hire workers
   - Assign jobs
   - Manage supplies
   - Set work priorities
   - Control production
   - Manage money and wages
   - Watch workers and customers behave autonomously

2. ACTIVE PLAYER GAMEPLAY
   - Personally cook
   - Serve tables
   - Seat customers
   - Clean
   - Take orders
   - Step into a worker's role during busy periods

Anything a worker can eventually automate should also be something the player can temporarily perform manually.

The player should be able to run the game as:
- an architect,
- a manager,
- an active worker,
- or a mixture of all three.

The game should eventually support a tavern that can run almost entirely by itself, while still allowing the player to jump in whenever desired.

==================================================
3. FIRST DEMO FANTASY
==================================================

The player begins with a tiny medieval roadside tavern.

At first there may only be:

- a small dining room
- a small kitchen
- several tables and chairs
- storage
- an oven
- a brewing vat
- one or two workers
- a little starting gold

Customers arrive from the road.

They enter the tavern, wait for seating, order bread and beer, eat and drink, pay, tip, review the tavern, and leave.

The player uses profits to:

- buy more supplies
- hire workers
- expand the dining room
- expand the kitchen
- buy additional furniture
- improve storage
- improve production
- serve more customers

Eventually the small tavern can grow into a much larger medieval institution.


==================================================
4. THE ESTABLISHMENT IS THE COLONY
==================================================

The tavern should effectively function like the colony in RimWorld.

The establishment is the player's main organism.

The player watches:

- people move,
- resources move,
- goods get processed,
- jobs get generated,
- workers claim jobs,
- customers wait,
- products get served,
- money comes in,
- problems develop.

The tavern's purpose is serving outsiders.

This gives the game a clearer identity than simply being another restaurant tycoon.

==================================================
20. PHYSICAL LOGISTICS
==================================================

Items should exist physically in the world whenever practical.

For example, bread should not simply become "+1 Bread" globally.

Instead:

- ingredients are delivered
- ingredients sit in storage
- a worker carries them
- they are processed
- products appear
- products are moved
- waiters pick them up
- customers receive them

Distance should matter.

Example:

Kitchen -------- 18 tiles -------- Pantry

If the pantry is too far away, the cook wastes time walking.

Good layout becomes a major gameplay mechanic.

Important flows:

Storage → Prep
Prep → Oven
Oven → Serving Counter
Serving Counter → Tables
Tables → Dirty Dish Area
Dirty Dish Area → Sink
Sink → Clean Storage


==================================================
21. PRODUCTION RULES
==================================================

Production should eventually support RimWorld-style bills.

Example:

BAKE BREAD

Produce until:
20 Bread

Pause above:
20

Resume below:
10

Example:

BREW BEER

Maintain:
30 Beer

This means production happens before customers order.

Players must prepare for rushes.

==================================================
31. STRATEGIC CHOICE: BUY OR PRODUCE
==================================================

A major long-term management decision should be:

Do I buy processed goods for convenience,
or do I produce them myself for lower cost?

Example:

OPTION A:
Buy Flour
- More expensive
- No mill needed
- Less labor
- Less space
- Faster

OPTION B:
Buy Wheat and Mill It
- Cheaper input
- Requires mill
- Requires worker time
- Requires storage
- Requires space

OPTION C:
Grow Wheat
- Cheapest material cost
- Requires land
- Requires farmers
- Requires seasons/time
- Requires storage
- Requires milling
- Much larger operation

This gives natural progression without forcing one playstyle.

Prototype UI should be simple:

- grey panels
- plain buttons
- debug text
- simple icons

Final UI can later become:

- parchment
- wood
- iron
- wax seals
- medieval ledgers
- hand-painted icons

Do not spend large amounts of time polishing UI art before the simulation is fun.

40. CORE DESIGN PRINCIPLES
==================================================

1. EVERYTHING IMPORTANT SHOULD BE PHYSICAL WHEN PRACTICAL

Resources, products and workers should move through the world.

2. DISTANCE IS GAMEPLAY

Bad layouts create wasted walking and slower service.

3. AUTOMATION AND MANUAL PLAY SHOULD COEXIST

The player can automate a task or personally perform it.

4. CONTENT SHOULD BE DATA-DRIVEN

Recipes, furniture, workers, customers and items should be replaceable or expandable without rewriting core systems.

5. PROTOTYPE ART IS DISPOSABLE

Do not design the engine around temporary textures or sprites.

6. START SMALL

Bread + Beer are enough to prove most major systems.

7. BUILD FOR EXPANSION

The demo is a tavern, but the architecture should be capable of supporting a much larger medieval production/service economy.

8. THE TAVERN IS THE COLONY

The establishment itself is the main evolving system the player builds, manages and watches.


==================================================
41. FIRST MAJOR PLAYABLE TARGET
==================================================

The first truly playable version should allow this:

The player starts with a nearly empty medieval tavern and a limited amount of gold.

They buy flour, yeast, malt, hops and water.

A delivery arrives.

Workers haul the supplies into storage.

The player places an oven and brewing vat.

Bread and beer are produced as physical items.

Tables and chairs are placed.

Customers enter.

A host seats them.

They order bread and beer.

A waiter delivers the products.

Customers eat and drink.

They pay.

They leave tips.

They generate satisfaction and reviews.

The player receives money.

The player buys more supplies.

The tavern grows.

The player can hire more workers and expand the kitchen or dining room.

During a rush, the player can personally step in and perform jobs.

At the end of the day the tavern has either made a profit or suffered a loss.

That is the first complete game loop.


==================================================
42. LONG-TERM ECONOMIC VISION
==================================================

The player's progression should move naturally from:

BUYING EVERYTHING
    ↓
PROCESSING PURCHASED MATERIALS
    ↓
PROCESSING LESS-REFINED MATERIALS
    ↓
PRODUCING RAW MATERIALS
    ↓
VERTICAL INTEGRATION
    ↓
SPECIALIZATION / TRADE

Example bread evolution:

Buy Bread
    ↓
Buy Flour → Bake Bread
    ↓
Buy Wheat → Mill Flour → Bake Bread
    ↓
Grow Wheat → Mill Flour → Bake Bread

Example beer evolution:

Buy Beer
    ↓
Buy Malt + Hops → Brew Beer
    ↓
Buy Barley → Produce Malt → Brew Beer
    ↓
Grow Barley + Grow Hops → Brew Beer

The player should never be forced to produce everything personally.

Buying, producing, specializing and trading should all remain valid strategies.
