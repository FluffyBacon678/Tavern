## 1. Targets: high-end phones and Steam

The game ships on both, high-end only.

| Platform | Minimum | Measured so far |
|---|---|---|
| PC (Steam) | This laptop: Dell Pro 14 Plus, Intel Core Ultra 7 268V, Arc 140V (integrated), 32 GB | Test house 16.7 ms median at 5× (1080p). Largest tavern 17.1 ms median, 30.9 ms p95. |
| Phones | Current flagships: Snapdragon 8 Elite / 8 Gen 3 Android, iPhone 15 Pro (A17 Pro) and newer | Never built or run. |

What this asks of the work:

- **Frame budget:** 60 fps on the laptop in the largest tavern, which means
  bringing that 30.9 ms p95 under 16.7 ms. Phones should hold 60 fps, with 30
  as the floor once they are hot.
- **An Android build early,** before more content: export, install, measure
  frame time, heat, memory and load time. iOS needs a Mac, Xcode and an Apple
  developer account; plan for it, but build Android first.
- **Renderer:** the game uses Compatibility (OpenGL). Measure the Mobile
  (Vulkan) renderer on the phone before choosing.
- **Touch from the start of every UI change:**
  - Tap to inspect; one finger pans; pinch zooms; two fingers turn.
  - Hit targets of at least 9 mm.
  - Nothing that only shows on hover: every tooltip needs a tap or long-press.
  - The build bar and the cards need a phone layout.
- **Phone lifecycle:** pause and save when the app goes to the background;
  restore cleanly after the system kills it.
- **Steam, later:** Steamworks (GodotSteam, MIT licence) for cloud saves and
  achievements, store-page art, Steam Deck as a nice-to-have. Not before the
  demo loop is proven.

## 2. Weak parts to work on

Status is as of 2026-10-04. Items marked *confirm* wait on the owner's test
report before work starts.

### Gameplay

1. **The kitchen is mostly hauling.** On day 1 of the sandbox, cooks spent
   about 70% of their time carrying (water alone 28%) and 15% cooking. A few
   guests a day find nothing to order, and the bar's lemonade waits on water
   held at other benches. Fix: the owner's carrying rule above, measured with
   the persona players over several seeds.
2. **Service is one trip per thing ordered.** A plate and a mug are two waiter
   trips, and far tables (gardens) slow the whole house. *Confirm.*
3. **Diagnosis is one line.** The advice names the biggest loss, but not where
   an order is stuck or what to change (roadmap item). *Confirm.*
4. **The tutorial is long** (65 steps) and reaches service late; the roadmap
   wants a first paid beer within about five minutes.
5. **Profit has no purpose yet:** nothing to save up for (roadmap order 3).
6. **Guests aren't memorable:** the six types differ in behaviour, but nobody
   returns or is recognized.

### Visuals

7. **The Bar Table still looks like a lemonade market stall** (striped awning,
   lemon sign).
8. **Walk-up guests carry nothing visible** back from the bar.
9. **People overlap and bunch** when given the same errand; there is no
   avoidance.
10. **No working animations:** stirring, pouring, sweeping, carrying poses.
11. **Interiors are dark** at the default camera angle; lighting balance.
12. **Characters short of the reference art** (proportions, faces, animation).

13. **Gardens:** done today. Four new flower beds (tulips, lavender, roses,

### Presentation and technical

15. **Sound is thin:** an audio pass is unmeasured.

## 3. Play as your keeper

**Goal:** one button (and a key) turns management into playing your keeper,
seamlessly. The camera glides down to the keeper, and from then the mouse
moves them exactly like RuneScape. Press it again and the management view
returns exactly as it was. What the keeper can *do* comes later, designed by
the owner.

**Status 2026-10-04:** stages A–E are built, and stage F has begun (see
[KEEPER.md](KEEPER.md)). The keeper can take and carry goods, make any recipe
by hand, fish with the basic rod, clear tables, wash up, and make a prep table
or bar a **till** where guests buy and pay with no waiter. With the whole staff
let go, the keeper alone kept the sandbox trading at about +115g a day.

### Stages

| Stage | What it adds | Done when |
|---|---|---|
| A. The keeper in the world | The keeper you created stands in the tavern as a real person: idles by the bar, saved where they stand, paid no wage, takes no jobs yet. | Visible, saved and restored. Staff and guests walk round them. |
| B. The switch | A "Play as keeper" button in the toolbar, plus a key (Tab). The camera eases over about 0.6 s to follow the keeper, closer in. The HUD shrinks to clock, purse and a return button. Building pauses; time keeps its speed. | One press each way, no hitch. The management camera returns exactly where it was. |
| C. RuneScape movement | Left-click ground: walk there along the same paths staff use, with a yellow cross at the spot; clicking again re-routes. Right-click opens "Walk here / Examine / Cancel". Middle-drag or the arrow keys turn the camera round the keeper; the wheel zooms. Walls near the keeper cut away. Phones: tap to walk. | The keeper always walks to the clicked tile by the shortest open route. UI clicks never move them. |
| D. Hover and examine | RuneScape-style hover text ("Walk here", "Examine Ranger") and examine lines from the existing inspection cards. | Every person and piece has an examine line. |
| E. Saves | Saves keep the mode, the keeper's position and the camera. | Save and load in either mode resume exactly. |
| F. Things to do (later) | The owner's design: serve from the bar, carry stock, cook a batch by hand (reusing "Do it yourself"), greet and talk to guests, wear wardrobe items, step off the plot into the wider world. | — |

### Technical notes

- The keeper is a `Pawn` with the owner's appearance, moved by the simulation
  clock like everyone else, so speed controls and saves just work.
- The camera rig already follows a person (F); the switch reuses it with a
  closer framing.
- `WorldInput` sends clicks to inspection today. In play mode a left click on
  the ground becomes a move order instead.
- Keeper actions must go through the same jobs and goods as staff, so books
  and goods always reconcile.
